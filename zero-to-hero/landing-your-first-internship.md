# landing your first internship

The actual mechanics of finding and landing an internship, once you know roughly what you're aiming for. If you haven't picked a direction yet, start with [[picking-your-endgame]] first.

## the prerequisites, in order

It's easy to feel like you need everything at once: a perfect resume, LeetCode mastery, a full portfolio, a LinkedIn full of connections. You don't, and trying to build all of it simultaneously is how people freeze up and do nothing. Build these up roughly in this order:

1. **Something to point to.** A GitHub with at least one real, finished project is the actual "sauce" that gets you taken seriously. This comes before almost everything else, a great resume with nothing behind it falls apart the moment someone asks a follow-up question about it.
2. **Technical prep.** LeetCode and real pattern recognition, so you can pass the technical screen once you get one. Practice solving, then re-solving problems until you could walk from brute force to optimized live, not just grinding for volume.
3. **Behavioral prep.** Once you can clear the technical bar, the interviewer's actual question becomes "do I want to work with this person." See [[interview-guide]] for the STAR/behavioral mechanics, no need to duplicate it here.
4. **Resume.** Turn the project and the prep into a document that gets you the interview in the first place. See [[resume-guide]] for the full breakdown.
5. **LinkedIn.** Your resume gets you through an application, your LinkedIn is what a recruiter or a warm contact actually checks before reaching out. See [[linkedin-guide]].

None of this is a strict gate, you can work on more than one at once. But if you only have time for one thing this week, work on whichever one is lowest on this list that you still don't have.

## increasing your luck surface area

If you don't have connections or a project yet, hackathons solve both at once, you walk away with something built and people you built it with. If you don't have networking opportunities yet, cold outreach works, see [[referrals-guide]] for how to actually do it well instead of just spamming messages. And if "software engineer intern" listings specifically aren't converting, apply to less competitive-sounding titles too: data scientist/analyst, solutions engineer, PM, security engineer, UI engineer. They're real experience, and often lead right back to a SWE role later.

## researching companies

Before you apply anywhere, a few minutes on Glassdoor, levels.fyi, and relevant subreddits tells you more about actual comp, culture, and interview process than the job posting ever will. Use it to prioritize where you spend your limited application time, not just to decide where to apply after the fact.
